Day 1 · Protection basics

What is a CT and how does it work?

Protection relays cannot sit on a 33 kV line carrying 600 A. A current transformer (CT) solves this: it reproduces the line current as a small, safe, proportional current, usually 5 A or 1 A, that the relay can measure.

The ratio

A CT is labelled by its ratio, for example 100/5 A. When 100 A flows in the primary, 5 A flows in the secondary, so the secondary current is the primary current divided by 20.

Secondary current: 30.0 A

Safety: never open a live CT secondary

A CT works as a current source. If its secondary circuit is opened while primary current flows, the core saturates and a dangerously high voltage appears across the open terminals. Always short the CT secondary before disconnecting a relay, and follow your site procedure.
